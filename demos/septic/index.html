Slow drains

What it can mean

Usually a full tank or a clog in the line between the house and the tank. If every drain is slow, look at the tank. If it's one sink, it's likely just that pipe.

What to do now

Hold off on laundry and long showers until we look. Skip the drain cleaner, it's hard on the tank. If water backs up into a tub or floor drain, call us.

Septic inspections

For your own records, after a problem, or because it's been a while. We open the tank, check the levels and the parts, walk the drain field, and explain what we found in plain words.

Buying or selling a home?

Tell us the closing date and who needs the report. You'll get a written inspection the buyer, the seller and the lender can all read. Book early; the last few days before closing get tight.

Repairs and risers

Cracked lids, broken baffles, the pipe between the house and the tank. Risers bring your lid up to ground level, so the next pump-out needs no digging.

Drain field check

Wet spots, smells, or slow drains even after pumping. We check whether the field is taking water and lay out your options.

Where's my tank?

We find it, mark it, and draw it on a sketch you keep with your house papers. Handy for you and whoever owns the house next.

Before we arrive

A few small things make the visit quicker, and quicker usually means it costs less.

  • Clear around the lid

    Move cars, firewood or planters off the area. If you don't know where it is, that's fine. We'll find it.

  • Keep pets inside

    An open tank is no place for a curious dog. The same goes for kids.

  • Find any records

    An old pumping receipt or the county permit drawing tells us a lot. A phone photo works.

  • Leave the gate open

    Not home? Leave it unlocked, or put the code in your request. We'll text you when we're done.

Questions we get a lot

Don't see yours? Call. Questions are free.

What shouldn't go down the drain?

Toilet paper and what your body makes. That's the list. Keep these out:

  • Wipes, even the ones labeled "flushable"
  • Grease and cooking oil
  • Paper towels and feminine products
  • Cat litter, medicine, paint and harsh cleaners
Do septic additives help?

Our honest view: most systems don't need them. A working tank already has the bacteria it needs. Additives don't replace pumping, and some can stir solids out toward the drain field. We'd rather you save that money for a regular pump-out.

How long does a pump-out take?

Usually about an hour once we can reach the lid. If we have to dig to find it, add some time. That's why we suggest risers: next time, no digging.

Do I need to be home?

Not for a routine pump-out. If we can get to the lid, the gate is open and pets are inside, we can do it while you're at work. We'll call on the way and text you what we found. For inspections and repairs, it helps if someone's there.

I have no idea where my tank is. Is that a problem?

Not at all. Your county may have a permit drawing on file, so that's worth a call. You can also look for a patch where the grass grows greener or snow melts first. Or let us find it, and we'll mark it for next time.
