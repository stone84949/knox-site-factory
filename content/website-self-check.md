# Can a customer take the next step on your website?

Open your website on your phone. Imagine someone nearby has been given your business name and is deciding whether to call. Use this checklist to see what they can learn and do.

You do not need a score. Mark each item **works**, **needs attention**, or **not relevant**, and write down what you actually see.

## 1. Say what you do and where you work

- [ ] The opening screen names the main service in plain language.
- [ ] A customer can find the towns or area you serve without opening several pages.
- [ ] Your business name is consistent across the site.

Try asking someone who has never seen the site: “What does this business do, and where does it work?” If the answer is vague, start with the headline and service-area wording.

## 2. Make the phone number usable

- [ ] A call button is easy to find on a phone.
- [ ] Tapping it opens the dialer with the correct number. Cancel before placing the call.
- [ ] The number matches on the home page, contact page, and footer.
- [ ] Hours and any after-hours limits reflect how you actually operate.

Only describe emergency or round-the-clock service if your team provides it.

## 3. Explain the service before asking for the job

- [ ] Your main services have a short, understandable explanation.
- [ ] Repair, replacement, and maintenance are distinguished where that matters.
- [ ] Any prices, promotions, and expiration dates are current.
- [ ] The customer can tell what happens after an inquiry.

“Tell us what is happening; our office will discuss the next step” is useful if it matches your process. An exact arrival-time promise belongs on the page only when you can keep it.

## 4. Show proof you can stand behind

- [ ] Photos show your actual people or work, or are clearly labeled illustrations.
- [ ] Testimonials are genuine and used with permission.
- [ ] License, insurance, experience, and certification statements are current and accurate.
- [ ] There are no sample reviews or placeholder claims left in the published page.

If a claim cannot be checked, leave it out until you can verify it.

## 5. Check the inquiry path end to end

- [ ] The form asks for information you need to respond.
- [ ] Required fields and errors are clear.
- [ ] You can complete it on a phone without sideways scrolling or obscured buttons.
- [ ] A clearly labeled test inquiry arrives in the right inbox.
- [ ] Someone knows who responds and how to spot missed inquiries.

If you own or manage the site, arrange a test with the recipient first. A thank-you message alone does not confirm delivery. Avoid submitting test requests to someone else's business.

## Pick one fix

Start with anything that prevents a customer from contacting you: a wrong number, a broken call button, or an inquiry that never arrives. Then make your service and service area clear. Save decorative changes until those basics work.
