# Website self-check: companion content

## Short article

### Before a website redesign, test the call button

If you run a home-service business, try a small website check before deciding what to redesign.

Open your home page on your phone. Can you tell what service you offer and where you work? Can you find the phone number? Tap the call button and check the number in the dialer, then cancel the call.

Next, visit your contact page. Does it show the same number? Do the hours match when someone can actually respond? Look for old promotions, sample text, and promises your office no longer makes.

If you have an inquiry form, arrange a test with the person who receives it. Send a clearly labeled test request and confirm it reaches the right inbox. Check who follows up. The thank-you screen is only one part of that process.

Write down what you find before changing anything. If the number is wrong, fix it and retest it. If the service area is unclear, name the places you actually serve. If inquiries are getting lost, trace the delivery path with whoever manages the site.

A redesign may still be useful. These checks give you a concrete starting point and help you explain what the next version needs to do.

Use the accompanying website self-check to record the issues, choose one fix, and check the result afterward.

## Social caption 2 — form delivery

When did you last check where your website inquiries go?

Arrange a test with the person who receives them. Submit a clearly labeled test, confirm it arrived, and check who responds. A thank-you screen does not tell you whether the office got the message.

Add that check to your routine whenever the form or its destination changes.

## Social caption 3 — clear service areas

Ask someone unfamiliar with your website to open it on a phone: “What do we do, and where do we work?”

If they cannot answer, try a clearer opening sentence and an easy-to-find service-area description. Use the towns you actually serve and the language your customers use when they call.
