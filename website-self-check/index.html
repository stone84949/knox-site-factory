Can a customer take the next step?

Open your website on your phone. Imagine someone nearby has been given your business name and is deciding whether to call. Use these checks to see what they can learn and do.

Check an item when it works. Leave it unchecked if it needs attention or is not relevant. Write your observations on a printed copy.

Say what you do and where you work

Ask someone who has never seen the site: “What does this business do, and where does it work?” If the answer is vague, start with the headline and service-area wording.

Observation:

Make the phone number usable

Only describe emergency or round-the-clock service if your team provides it.

Observation:

Explain the service before asking for the job

“Tell us what is happening; our office will discuss the next step” is useful if it matches your process. An exact arrival-time promise belongs on the page only when you can keep it.

Observation:

Show proof you can stand behind

If a claim cannot be checked, leave it out until you can verify it.

Observation:

Check the inquiry path end to end

If you own or manage the site, arrange a test with the recipient first. A thank-you message alone does not confirm delivery. Avoid submitting test requests to someone else’s business.

Observation:

Pick one fix

Start with anything that prevents a customer from contacting you: a wrong number, a broken call button, or an inquiry that never arrives. Then make your service and service area clear. Save decorative changes until those basics work.
