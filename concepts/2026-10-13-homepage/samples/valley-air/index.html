How a visit works

No surprises, in this order, every time.

  1. You call or send a request

    Tell us what the system is doing. We'll ask a few questions so the right parts are on the truck.

  2. We set a time and the fee

    You get a two-hour arrival window and the visit fee before we hang up. We text when we're on the way.

  3. We find the problem

    The tech shows you what failed, with photos if it's somewhere you can't see, and gives you the price in writing.

  4. You decide

    Nothing gets fixed until you say yes. If you'd rather think it over, that's fine too.

If we can't fix it today, you'll know why, what it costs, and when we'll be back.

Questions people ask before they call

Still not sure? Call 865-555-0101 and ask. It's free to ask.

Do you charge to come out?

Yes, there's a visit fee that covers the trip and finding the problem. We tell you the exact amount on the phone before we book, so it's never a surprise on the invoice.

How often should I change my filter?

For the common 1-inch filters, every 1 to 3 months. Go closer to monthly if you have pets, allergies, or the system runs hard in July and August. Thicker 4- or 5-inch filters usually last 6 months or more.

Write the date on the edge of the filter with a marker. It's the easiest way to remember.

Should I repair it or replace it?

A rough rule: if the system is over about 12 to 15 years old and the repair costs more than a third of a new one, replacing usually makes more sense. Under that, repair is often the better deal.

We'll give you both numbers and let you pick. Plenty of our replacement estimates end with us fixing the old one.

My AC is running but the house isn't cooling. Is it broken?

Not always. Check that the filter isn't clogged and the outdoor unit isn't buried in leaves or grass clippings. If there's ice on the copper line or the indoor coil, turn the system off and the fan to "On" to let it thaw, then call us. Running it frozen can hurt the compressor.

Do you work on all brands?

We service the brands you'll find in most East Tennessee homes. If yours needs a part only the maker's dealer can get, we'll say so on the first visit.
